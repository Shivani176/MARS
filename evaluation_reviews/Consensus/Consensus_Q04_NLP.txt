# **Recent Advances in Natural Language Processing (NLP):** A Brief Overview

Recent NLP progress is dominated by **transformer-based pretrained and large language models**, new **prompt-based learning paradigms**, and extensions to **multimodal and continual learning**. These advances emphasize scaling, transfer learning, and using prompts instead of task‑specific training.

## **Foundation & Large Language Models**

- Transformer-based pretrained models (GPT, BERT and successors) learn universal language representations via self‑supervised pretraining and transfer them to many tasks, avoiding training from scratch  (Kalyan et al., 2021; Ramprasath et al., 2022; Singh & Mahmood, 2021).  
- Scaling to **large language models (LLMs)** like GPT‑3 enables strong **few-shot and zero-shot** performance across translation, QA, arithmetic, and reasoning tasks without gradient updates  (Brown et al., 2020; Kalyan, 2023; Patil & Gudivada, 2024).  
- Surveys describe LLMs evolving from task‑specific to **task- and language-independent** architectures, with sizes reaching trillions of parameters and strong performance in both understanding and generation  (Patil & Gudivada, 2024; Souza et al., 2020; Singh & Mahmood, 2021).

## **Prompt-Based and In-Context Learning**

- The field is shifting from “pre-train, fine-tune” to **“pre-train, prompt, and predict”**, where tasks are reformulated as text prompts to exploit pretrained LMs directly  (Liu et al., 2021; Kalyan et al., 2021).  
- Systematic work on **prompt engineering** (discrete/continuous, few‑shot, zero‑shot) shows prompts are crucial for performance and can be optimized automatically  (Liu et al., 2021; Gao et al., 2021; Li, 2023).  
- Simple natural-language prompts and chain-of-thought instructions dramatically improve **zero-shot reasoning**  (Kojima et al., 2022)and clinical information extraction, often surpassing older neural baselines  (Sivarajkumar et al., 2024).  
- Studies also explore **zero-shot prompt design** and “prompt programming” beyond few-shot examples, emphasizing narrative and instruction-like prompts  (Reynolds & McDonell, 2021; Li, 2023).

## **Transfer, Multimodal, and Continual Learning**

- Transfer learning with pretrained transformers has become “necessary” for progress across domains like healthcare and legal text, improving accuracy and efficiency while reducing labeled data needs  (Joseph et al., 2024; Kalyan et al., 2021; Singh & Mahmood, 2021).  
- **Vision–language and multimodal models** combine text with images/video; prompt learning for CLIP and multimodal prompt tuning improve zero-shot image recognition and instruction following  (Zhou et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2024; Zeng et al., 2022).  
- **Continual learning** methods are being adapted to foundation models to mitigate catastrophic forgetting while they learn new tasks over time  (Yang et al., 2024).

## **Summary**

Modern NLP is driven by large transformer-based models, extensive self-supervised pretraining, and powerful prompt-based interfaces that enable zero- or few-shot use. Research is rapidly extending these models across languages, domains, and modalities, while tackling efficiency and continual learning so they can be adapted and reused widely.
