# **Deep learning techniques and where they are used**

Deep learning is a family of neural‑network methods that automatically learn useful features from large amounts of data, and now underpins many modern AI systems across science, industry, and government  (Alzubaidi et al., 2021; Ahmed et al., 2023; Alom et al., 2019; Sarker, 2021; Mienye & Swart, 2024).

## **Core Techniques and Model Families**

### Supervised / discriminative models
- **Feedforward / Deep Neural Networks (DNN, MLP)** for general prediction and classification tasks  (Alom et al., 2019; Sarker, 2021; Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019).  
- **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)** for images, video, and other grid‑like data; foundation of modern computer vision  (Alzubaidi et al., 2021; Ahmed et al., 2023; Alom et al., 2019; Abdel-Jaber et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2017; Mienye & Swart, 2024).  
- **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), LSTM, GRU** for sequences such as text, speech, and time series  (Ahmed et al., 2023; Alom et al., 2019; Alom et al., 2018; Alhijaj & Khudeyer, 2023; Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019).  
- **Transformers and attention models** for language understanding, translation, and other sequential/structured data  (Sarker, 2021; Abdel-Jaber et al., 2022; Mienye & Swart, 2024).

### Unsupervised / generative models
- **Autoencoders, variational autoencoders** for feature learning, compression, denoising  (Dong et al., 2021; Sarker, 2021; Abdel-Jaber et al., 2022; Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019).  
- **Restricted Boltzmann Machines, Deep Belief Networks, Deep Boltzmann Machines** as early deep generative models  (Dong et al., 2021; Alom et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2017; Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019).  
- **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)** for realistic image, audio, and data generation  (Ahmed et al., 2023; Alom et al., 2019; Sarker, 2021; Abdel-Jaber et al., 2022; Mienye & Swart, 2024).  
- **Capsule networks, graph neural networks (GNNs)** as newer architectures for structured and relational data  (Ahmed et al., 2023; Abdel-Jaber et al., 2022; Mienye & Swart, 2024).

### Other important paradigms
- **Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL)** for decision‑making and control (e.g., robotics, games)  (Alom et al., 2019; Alom et al., 2018; Mienye & Swart, 2024; Vaissnave et al., 2024).  
- **Transfer, self‑supervised, and federated learning** to reuse knowledge, exploit unlabeled data, and train across distributed datasets  (Alom et al., 2019; Sarker, 2021; Mienye & Swart, 2024; Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019).

## **Advantages, Challenges, and Trends**

- **Strengths:** Learns high‑level representations from raw data, excels on large and complex datasets, often surpasses traditional machine learning  (Alzubaidi et al., 2021; Ahmed et al., 2023; Alom et al., 2019; Sarker, 2021; Mienye & Swart, 2024; Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019; Shiri et al., 2023).  
- **Challenges:** High data and compute demands; training can be slow, expensive, and prone to overfitting or local minima; models are often “black boxes”  (Alzubaidi et al., 2021; Ahmed et al., 2023; Sarker, 2021; Abdel-Jaber et al., 2022; Mienye & Swart, 2024; Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019).  
- **Trends:** Hybrid and ensemble models, automated model search, more interpretable and efficient architectures, and expansion into new domains like finance, agriculture, and advanced robotics  (Ahmed et al., 2023; Sarker, 2021; Esteva et al., 2019; Mienye & Swart, 2024; Vaissnave et al., 2024; Soori et al., 2023; Górriz et al., 2023).

## **Summary**

Key deep learning techniques include CNNs, RNN/LSTMs, transformers, autoencoders, GANs, and DRL, used in supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement settings. They power applications across vision, language, speech, healthcare, cybersecurity, and robotics, but require large data, substantial compute, and better interpretability to fully meet real‑world needs.
