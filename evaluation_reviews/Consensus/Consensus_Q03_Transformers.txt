# **Transformer Architectures in Simple Terms:** Core Ideas and How They Work

Transformers are a type of deep neural network that became the dominant architecture for tasks involving sequences like text, images, and genomic data. They replace older recurrent or convolutional sequence models with a design built almost entirely around **attention**.

## **Core Architecture and Building Blocks**

Transformers are typically made by stacking identical **blocks** that each contain:  
- **Multi-head self-attention**: lets every element in a sequence “look at” every other element and decide how important it is. This is what captures **long-range dependencies** regardless of distance in the sequence  (Tay et al., 2020; Vaswani et al., 2017; Khan et al., 2021).  
- **Position-wise feed‑forward network**: a small neural network applied separately to each position to transform the attended information  (Choi & Lee, 2023; Tay et al., 2020; Khan et al., 2021).  
- **Residual connections and layer normalization**: help stabilize and speed up training by adding each sublayer’s input to its output, then normalizing  (Rahali & Akhloufi, 2023; Tay et al., 2020; Khan et al., 2021).

Positional encodings are added so the model knows the order of tokens, since attention itself is order-agnostic  (Tay et al., 2020; Singh & Mahmood, 2021; Wolf et al., 2019).

### Transformer Variants by Structure

- **Encoder–decoder (sequence‑to‑sequence)**: original design for machine translation; encoder reads input, decoder generates output step‑by‑step using attention to encoder outputs  (Singh & Mahmood, 2021; Vaswani et al., 2017).  
- **Encoder‑only** (e.g., BERT): good for understanding tasks like classification and question answering  (Rahali & Akhloufi, 2023; Țucudean et al., 2024; Ghojogh & Ghodsi, 2020; Zhang & Shafiq, 2024).  
- **Decoder‑only** (e.g., GPT): good for **generation**, predicting the next token given previous ones  (Rahali & Akhloufi, 2023; Ghojogh & Ghodsi, 2020; Yenduri et al., 2023; Zhang & Shafiq, 2024).

## **How Attention Works Conceptually**

Self‑attention scores how much each token should attend to others, then forms a weighted combination of their representations. Multiple “heads” learn different types of relationships in parallel  (Tay et al., 2020; Vaswani et al., 2017; Khan et al., 2021).  
Visualization tools show that heads often learn meaningful structure (e.g., grammar in text, regions in images, or genomic features) and that patterns evolve across layers  (Yeh et al., 2023; Vig, 2019; Consens et al., 2025).

## **Efficiency and Extensions**

Standard attention is costly on long sequences, so many “efficient transformers” approximate or modify attention to reduce memory and time, such as linear attention (e.g., Performer) and other variants  (Rahali & Akhloufi, 2023; Tay et al., 2020). Compression, pruning, and quantization are also used to run models on limited hardware  (Rahali & Akhloufi, 2023; Han et al., 2020; Singh & Mahmood, 2021).

## **Conclusion**

Transformers are stacks of attention‑based blocks that process all positions in parallel, use attention to model relationships across entire sequences, and rely on simple feed‑forward and normalization layers around that core. Variants of the basic encoder/decoder design underpin models like BERT and GPT and have been adapted across text, vision, and genomics.
