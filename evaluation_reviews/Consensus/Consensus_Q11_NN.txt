# **Fundamental Concepts and Types of Neural Networks**

Neural networks are computational models inspired by the brain, built from many simple processing units (“neurons”) connected in layers. They learn patterns from data by adjusting connection weights to reduce error on a task.

## **Core Concepts**

- **Neurons and Layers**: Networks consist of input, hidden, and output layers of artificial neurons. Each neuron sums its inputs (weighted by learned parameters) plus a bias and passes this through a non‑linear **activation function** such as sigmoid, tanh, or ReLU to capture complex patterns  (Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019; López et al., 2022; Ghosh et al., 2019; Thakur, 2021).  
- **Topology / Architecture**: The **topology** describes how neurons and layers are organized and connected (feedforward vs recurrent, number of layers, presence of intra‑ or supra‑layer connections) and strongly affects performance  (López et al., 2022).  
- **Learning and Backpropagation**: Networks are trained by minimizing a **loss function** using gradient‑based optimization; backpropagation computes gradients of the loss with respect to all weights  (Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019; López et al., 2022; Hagan et al., 1995; Hammad, 2024).  
- **Shallow vs Deep**: Deep neural networks have multiple hidden layers (multiple non‑linear transformations), enabling hierarchical feature learning from raw data  (Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019; Emmert-Streib et al., 2020; Alzubaidi et al., 2021).

## **Major Types of Neural Networks**

### **Feedforward / Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)**  
Information flows one way from input to output with no cycles. Used for general regression and classification and considered the basic network model  (Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019; López et al., 2022; Emmert-Streib et al., 2020; Ghosh et al., 2019; Sharkawy, 2020; Thakur, 2021).

### **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)**  
Introduce local connectivity, shared weights (filters), and pooling to learn translation‑robust features, especially for images and other multidimensional signals  (Gonzalez, 2018; Du, 2024; Ghosh et al., 2019; Alzubaidi et al., 2021; Taye, 2023; Purwono et al., 2023).

### **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Variants**  
Include feedback connections, making them suitable for sequences and time series; important variants include LSTM and GRU networks  (Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019; Pan, 2024; Emmert-Streib et al., 2020; Sharkawy, 2020; DiPietro & Hager, 2019; Alzubaidi et al., 2021).

### **Other Common Types**  
- **Radial Basis Function (RBF) networks**: Use radial basis activations, with distinct advantages and disadvantages vs MLPs  (Ghosh et al., 2019; Sharkawy, 2020).  
- **Autoencoders and Variational Autoencoders**: Learn compressed representations and generative models  (Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019; Makosso et al., 2025; Emmert-Streib et al., 2020; Alzubaidi et al., 2021).  
- **Deep reinforcement learning architectures**: Combine neural nets with reinforcement learning for decision‑making tasks  (Shrestha & Mahmood, 2019; Makosso et al., 2025; Alzubaidi et al., 2021).

## **Conclusion**

Fundamental neural network ideas center on layers of simple nonlinear units, their connectivity (topology), and gradient‑based learning. The main families—feedforward, convolutional, recurrent, RBF, and autoencoder‑based networks—differ primarily in how they connect neurons and handle structure in data (spatial, temporal, or latent), and these design choices determine which problems each type is best suited to solve.
