# **Vision Transformers in Computer Vision:** Core Ideas and Mechanics

Vision Transformers (ViTs) adapt the transformer architecture from language to images by turning images into sequences and using self-attention instead of convolutions. They have become strong backbones for many vision tasks, from classification to detection and segmentation  (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020; Khan et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2021; Li et al., 2023; Jamil et al., 2022).

## **Core Working Principle**

### From image to tokens  
- Images are **split into small patches** (e.g., a grid)  (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020; Jamil et al., 2022; Bazi et al., 2021).  
- Each patch is **flattened and linearly projected** into a vector; this becomes a “token”, analogous to a word embedding in NLP  (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020; Jamil et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2021).  
- **Positional embeddings** are added so the model knows where each patch came from in the original image  (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020; Bazi et al., 2021; Hassija et al., 2025).

### Transformer encoding  
- The sequence of patch tokens is passed through a **Transformer encoder** with multi-head self-attention and feed-forward layers  (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020; Khan et al., 2021; Han et al., 2020).  
- **Self-attention** lets each patch weight information from all other patches, capturing **global context and long-range dependencies** across the image  (Khan et al., 2021; Han et al., 2020; Jamil et al., 2022).  
- A special **classification token** or pooled representation is sent to an MLP head to output class labels or other predictions  (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020; Jamil et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2025).

## **How This Differs from CNNs**

| Aspect | CNNs | Vision Transformers | Citations |
|-------|------|---------------------|-----------|
| Inductive bias | Strong (locality, translation equivariance) | Minimal, more general |  (Han et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021; Khan et al., 2021)|
| Context | Mainly local, builds up with depth | Direct **global** interactions via attention |  (Khan et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2021; Li et al., 2023)|
| Input handling | Pixels / small kernels | Image **patch tokens** + positions |  (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020; Jamil et al., 2022; Bazi et al., 2021)|

## **Applications Across Vision Tasks**

- **Image classification:** ViT and its variants achieve state‑of‑the‑art results on benchmarks like ImageNet  (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2025; Jamil et al., 2022).  
- **Detection & segmentation:** DETR and ViT-based backbones replace anchors and heavy CNN heads with query-based and attention-based designs  (Li et al., 2023; Beal et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021).  
- **Low-level and 3D tasks:** Used for super-resolution, enhancement, colorization, point cloud analysis, and medical imaging  (Khan et al., 2021; Azad et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023; Pu et al., 2024).  
- **Hybrid models:** CNN–Transformer hybrids combine local convolutional features with global self-attention to capture both local and global structure  (Khan et al., 2023; Papa et al., 2023; Elharrouss et al., 2025).

## **Conclusion**

Vision Transformers work by converting images into sequences of patch tokens and using self-attention to model relationships across the entire image. This design reduces vision-specific assumptions, captures global context efficiently, and scales well, enabling strong performance across many computer vision tasks.
