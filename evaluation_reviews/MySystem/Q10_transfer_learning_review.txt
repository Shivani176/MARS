# Transfer Learning: A Structured Literature Review

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Transfer learning has emerged as a fundamental paradigm in machine learning, enabling models to leverage knowledge from one domain or task to improve performance on related tasks. This approach has found applications across diverse fields, from computer vision and natural language processing to specialized domains like electromyography and quantum machine learning [1,2,3].

## 2. KEY FINDINGS

### Domain-Specific Model Development and Performance

Language-specific models consistently outperform multilingual alternatives. Bangla-BERT achieved superior performance over multilingual BERT, improving state-of-the-art results by 3.52%, 2.2%, and 5.3% across different benchmark datasets [2]. Similarly, domain-specific BERT language model finetuning produced new state-of-the-art performance on the SemEval 2014 Task 4 restaurants dataset, with cross-domain adapted models performing significantly better than vanilla BERT-base and XLNet-base [19].

### Efficiency and Parameter Optimization

Transfer learning approaches demonstrate remarkable parameter efficiency across multiple domains. A Mamba model with 67 million parameters achieved performance comparable to a 307 million-parameter Vision Transformer model in zero-shot classification tasks [16]. Hybrid quantum-classical neural networks achieved similar sum rate performance to benchmark classical networks with significantly fewer training parameters [10].

### Cross-Domain Generalization

Several studies highlight the effectiveness of transfer learning for cross-domain applications. Point-BERT representations transfer well to new tasks and domains, largely advancing state-of-the-art performance in few-shot point cloud classification [7]. Dynamic Rank Adaptation (DRA) demonstrated superior performance in enhancing new class recognition across various benchmarks, including base-new classes, cross-datasets evaluation, and domain generalization [18].

### Multi-Modal and Multi-View Learning

Transfer learning proves particularly effective in multi-modal scenarios. Cross-view knowledge transfer frameworks successfully extract affordance-specific features from exocentric interactions and transfer them to egocentric views [5,6]. Texture Transformer Networks enable accurate texture transfer between reference and low-resolution images through attention mechanisms [12].

## 3. METHODOLOGICAL APPROACHES

### Pre-training Strategies

Self-supervised pre-training emerges as a dominant approach across domains. Point-BERT employs Masked Point Modeling with discrete Variational AutoEncoders to generate meaningful local information [7]. GPT-GNN introduces self-supervised attributed graph generation tasks, factorizing graph generation likelihood into attribute and edge generation components [13]. Foundation models utilize self-supervised learning combined with transfer learning to achieve human-like intelligence [3].

### Datasets and Evaluation

The literature employs diverse benchmark datasets for evaluation. EMG-based gesture recognition studies utilized Myo armband datasets with 19, 17, and 10 able-bodied participants respectively [1]. Bangla-BERT was trained on BanglaLM, constructed using 40 GB of text data [2]. Affordance grounding research constructed AGD20K dataset with over 20K images from 36 affordance categories [5,6].

### Architectural Innovations

Novel architectural components enhance transfer learning effectiveness. Correlation Alignment (CORAL) minimizes domain shift by aligning second-order statistics of source and target distributions without requiring target labels [14]. Latent Hierarchical Adapter (LatHAdapter) exploits latent semantic hierarchy through learnable attribute prompts and hyperbolic space projections [20].

## 4. RESEARCH GAPS

### Training Complexity and Stability

Mamba models exhibit sharper and more non-convex landscapes compared to ViT-based models, making them more challenging to train [16]. This suggests a need for improved training methodologies for state space models.

### Limited Cross-Domain Robustness

While some approaches show strong performance, cross-domain evaluation reveals limitations. Domain adaptation methods still struggle with significant domain shifts, particularly in unsupervised scenarios [14,19].

### Scalability Concerns

Deep learning algorithms in specialized domains like electromyography require unreasonable amounts of effort from single users to generate sufficient training examples [1]. This highlights the need for more efficient data collection and augmentation strategies.

### Integration Challenges

The literature reveals gaps in effectively combining different modalities and architectures. While hybrid approaches show promise [10], optimal integration strategies remain underexplored across different application domains.

## 5. CONCLUSION

The literature demonstrates that transfer learning consistently enhances performance across diverse domains while improving parameter efficiency and enabling cross-domain generalization. The field is moving toward more sophisticated pre-training strategies, multi-modal approaches, and domain-specific optimizations, though challenges remain in training stability, cross-domain robustness, and scalable implementation.
