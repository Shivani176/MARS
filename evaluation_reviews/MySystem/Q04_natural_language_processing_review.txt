# LITERATURE REVIEW: natural language processing

# A Literature Review on Natural Language Processing: Architectures, Applications, and Limitations

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Natural Language Processing has experienced significant advancement through the development of transformer architectures and large language models, fundamentally changing how machines process and understand human language [2][3]. These developments have enabled state-of-the-art performance across diverse NLP tasks while simultaneously raising questions about the true nature of language understanding in artificial systems [1].

## 2. KEY FINDINGS

### Transformer Architecture Dominance
The transformer architecture has emerged as the foundational framework for modern NLP, with the "Attention Is All You Need" paper introducing a revolutionary approach that relies heavily on attention mechanisms to process sequences, allowing for parallelization, efficient training, and the ability to capture long-range dependencies in data [3]. BERT, as a bidirectional encoder representation from transformers, has demonstrated exceptional performance by pre-training deep bidirectional representations from unlabeled text, achieving state-of-the-art results on eleven NLP tasks including pushing the GLUE score to 80.5% and SQuAD v1.1 question answering Test F1 to 93.2 [18].

### Performance Achievements and Model Capabilities
Large language models have demonstrated outstanding performance across a range of NLP tasks, with their ability to generate human-like language positioning them as highly sought-after research areas [6][9]. Specific applications have shown remarkable results, such as LSTM models achieving 87% accuracy in aviation safety report classification [5], and domain-specific models like Bangla-BERT surpassing state-of-the-art results by 3.52%, 2.2%, and 5.3% across different benchmark datasets [13].

### Prompt-Based Learning Paradigm
A new paradigm in NLP has emerged through prompt-based learning, which differs from traditional supervised learning by modifying the original input using templates into textual string prompts, allowing language models to perform few-shot or even zero-shot learning and adapt to new scenarios with minimal labeled data [4].

### Domain Adaptation and Language-Specific Models
Research consistently demonstrates that domain-specific and language-specific models outperform general multilingual approaches. Legal domain adaptation shows that both additional pretraining and vocabulary techniques enhance BERT model performance, with Legal-Vocab-BERT outperforming larger pretrained general language models [10]. Similarly, language-specific models like Cedille for French outperform existing French language models and remain competitive with GPT-3 on French zero-shot benchmarks [12].

## 3. METHODOLOGICAL APPROACHES

### Model Architectures and Training
The literature reveals diverse architectural approaches including LSTM, CNN, Bidirectional LSTM, and simple Recurrent Neural Networks for specific applications [5], while transformer-based models dominate general NLP tasks [15]. Pre-training methodologies involve massive datasets, with models like Bangla-BERT utilizing 40 GB of text data for language model construction [13].

### Attention Mechanisms
Beyond standard bi-attention frameworks, novel approaches like Tri-Attention explicitly incorporate context as a third dimension in calculating relevance scores, with four variants generated by expanding two-dimensional operations to tensor operations, demonstrating superior performance over 30 state-of-the-art approaches [11].

### Evaluation Frameworks
Models are consistently evaluated across standard benchmarks including GLUE, MultiNLI, SQuAD, and domain-specific datasets, with metrics focusing on accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 scores [5][13][18]. Cross-domain evaluation has emerged as a critical assessment method for real-world robustness [19].

## 4. RESEARCH GAPS

### Model Vulnerabilities and Limitations
Despite impressive performance, significant vulnerabilities exist in current models. BERT models show substantial vulnerability to input variation in Named Entity Recognition, with 20.2 to 45.0% of entities predicted completely wrong and another 29.3 to 53.3% predicted partially wrong, with models being particularly vulnerable to emergent entities [14]. Furthermore, large neural language models are argued to be ill-suited as comprehensive models of natural language, suggesting they do not represent a revolution in understanding cognition [1].

### Language and Cultural Representation
A critical gap exists in the representation of diverse languages, particularly African languages, where commercial large language models produce below-par performance across machine translation and text classification tasks [16]. This highlights the need for better representation of underrepresented languages in commercial systems.

### Resource Constraints and Accessibility
While transformer models show promise for low-resourced languages, achieving state-of-the-art results for six of ten South African languages [15], the resource-intensive nature of these models creates barriers for languages with limited computational resources and datasets [13].

## 5. CONCLUSION

The literature collectively demonstrates that while transformer-based architectures and large language models have achieved remarkable performance across diverse NLP tasks, fundamental limitations remain regarding true language understanding, model vulnerabilities, and equitable representation across languages and domains. The field continues to evolve rapidly with new paradigms like prompt-based learning, yet the gap between task performance and genuine linguistic comprehension persists as a central challenge.
