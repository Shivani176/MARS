# Literature Review: GPT Models and Applications

## INTRODUCTION

Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPTs) have emerged as a transformative technology in artificial intelligence, with GPT-4 exhibiting human-level performance on various professional and academic benchmarks [12]. The GPT ecosystem has evolved from foundational language models to encompass diverse applications, marketplaces, and research tools that are reshaping how we interact with AI systems [1,3].

## KEY FINDINGS

### Model Capabilities and Performance

GPT-4 demonstrates remarkable capabilities across multiple domains, solving novel tasks in mathematics, coding, vision, medicine, law, and psychology without special prompting, with performance strikingly close to human-level [10]. The model exhibits more general intelligence than previous AI models and could reasonably be viewed as an early version of artificial general intelligence (AGI) [10]. However, GPT-4V shows limitations, including failure to recognize simple Chinese texts and inconsistent performance on visual puzzle solving [11].

### Technical Scaling and Architecture

Significant advances have been made in scaling GPT architectures, with FoundationLayerNormalization enabling successful scaling of BERT and GPT models to 1,000 layers, representing an order of magnitude deeper than previous implementations [4]. For speech recognition applications, fine-tuned combinations of GPT and GPT-2 outperformed neural language models trained from scratch by up to 12% relative word error rate reduction [6].

### Ecosystem Development and Applications

The GPT Store serves as a dynamic marketplace for various GPT models, with categorization systems organizing GPTs by topics and user preferences influencing popularity [1]. Custom GPTs allow users to create specialized versions using specific instructions and knowledge to guide model behavior [5]. Research applications have expanded to include game theory experiments, where GPT exhibits human-like behaviors such as making positive offers in ultimatum games and demonstrating conditional cooperation in prisoner's dilemma scenarios [2].

### Security and Privacy Concerns

The GPT ecosystem faces significant security challenges, with custom GPTs posing privacy and security risks to users [5]. Third-party service integration in GPTs creates data privacy implications that are challenging to systematically evaluate due to limited accessibility of privacy setting information [8]. The GPT Store presents potential security threats that require evaluation of existing security measures [1].

## METHODOLOGICAL APPROACHES

Research methodologies span multiple approaches across the literature. Experimental studies utilize game theory frameworks with carefully designed prompts to enable GPT understanding of game rules and decision-making processes [2]. Technical evaluations employ manual construction of test instances, with one study creating 656 test instances to assess GPT-4V's performance across visual understanding, language comprehension, and multimodal tasks [11]. Automated frameworks have been developed for systematic data collection, such as GPTs-ThirdSpy for extracting privacy settings and analyzing third-party service integration [8]. Measurement studies focus on categorization analysis, popularity factor assessment, and security risk evaluation within the GPT Store ecosystem [1].

## RESEARCH GAPS

Several critical limitations and research needs emerge from the literature. GPT-4V demonstrates inconsistent refusal behavior when addressing sensitive traits such as gender, race, and age, and struggles with nuanced comparisons between similar images [11]. The mainstream BERT/GPT models contain only 10 to 20 layers, with little literature discussing deep model training [4]. Privacy setting information disclosure in GPT third-party services limits accessibility and systematic evaluation of data privacy implications [8]. There is a notable lack of standardized tools for systematically studying people's interactions with large language models despite their widespread use [3]. Additionally, advancing toward deeper AGI versions may require pursuing new paradigms beyond next-word prediction [10].

## CONCLUSION

The literature reveals GPT models as a rapidly evolving technology with demonstrated capabilities approaching artificial general intelligence, yet facing significant challenges in security, privacy, and systematic evaluation. The development of specialized tools, scaling techniques, and application frameworks indicates a maturing ecosystem, while identified limitations highlight the need for continued research in safety, standardization, and architectural innovation.
