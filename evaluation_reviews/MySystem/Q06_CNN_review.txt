# Literature Review: Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Their Integration with Attention Mechanisms and Transformers

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have established themselves as powerful models for various computer vision tasks, from image classification to object detection [1,8]. Recent developments have explored the integration of CNNs with attention mechanisms and Transformer architectures, leading to hybrid approaches that leverage the strengths of both paradigms [2,3,4,6,13].

## 2. KEY FINDINGS

### CNN Performance and Applications

CNNs demonstrate exceptional performance across diverse applications. In agricultural technology, CNN-based weed detection systems achieve remarkable accuracy, with the CNN LVQ model reaching 99.44% overall accuracy for weed detection using UAV imagery, including 100% user accuracy for soil class identification [1]. For facial expression recognition, CNNs with attention mechanisms (ACNNs) successfully handle occluded faces and improve recognition accuracy on both non-occluded and occluded faces [8].

### Integration of CNNs with Attention Mechanisms

The combination of CNNs with attention mechanisms yields significant performance improvements. In side-channel attacks, attention networks incorporating Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) outperform traditional ASCAD networks by focusing on relevant leakages while filtering out noise [3]. For physiological signal processing, CNN models with spatial attention mechanisms achieve best performance in classification problems, while channel attention mechanisms achieve lowest error in regression problems [4]. Importantly, CNN models with attention mechanisms demonstrate better performance and convergence than stand-alone self-attention models, indicating that convolutional operations and attention mechanisms are complementary [4].

### Hybrid CNN-Transformer Architectures

Several studies demonstrate successful integration of CNNs with Transformer architectures. In image dehazing, the DeHamer model combines CNN and Transformer through feature modulation, where CNN features are modulated via learning modulation matrices conditioned on Transformer features, achieving state-of-the-art performance [6]. For 3D reconstruction, the 3D-RETR model uses pretrained Transformers for visual feature extraction followed by CNN decoders for object reconstruction, reaching state-of-the-art performance [7]. Similarly, 3D-VRVT employs Vision Transformer encoders with CNN-based voxel decoders for effective 3D voxel reconstruction from both synthetic and real-world images [10].

### Enhanced Architectures and Novel Approaches

Advanced CNN architectures show superior performance through innovative designs. Pseudo-3D Residual Networks (P3D ResNet) simulate 3D convolutions using combinations of 2D and temporal convolutions, achieving improvements of 5.3% over 3D CNN and 1.8% over frame-based 2D CNN on Sports-1M dataset [2]. For satellite image object detection, SPH-YOLOv5 integrates Swin Transformer Prediction Heads and Normalization-based Attention Modules with YOLOv5, improving mean Average Precision by 0.071 on the DOTA dataset [13].

### Natural Language Processing Integration

In NLP applications, CNN architectures enhance sentence embedding models. When applied to BERT-based models, CNN sentence-embedding networks improve ALBERT models substantially more than BERT models for semantic textual similarity benchmarks, with ALBERT sentence embedding remaining highly competitive despite having significantly fewer parameters [5].

## 3. METHODOLOGICAL APPROACHES

The reviewed studies employ diverse datasets and evaluation metrics. Agricultural applications utilize UAV imagery datasets with 4400 images and 15336 segments [1]. Video processing studies leverage Sports-1M dataset for action recognition [2] and various MOT/MOTS datasets for object tracking [9]. Computer vision tasks employ standard benchmarks including ImageNet for classification [19], NWPU-VHR10 and DOTA datasets for satellite image detection [13], and RAF-DB and AffectNet for facial expression recognition [8].

Experimental designs commonly involve hyperparameter tuning and ablation studies. The CNN LVQ model uses snapshots for optimal parameter selection [1], while attention mechanism studies systematically compare different attention types (squeeze-and-excitation, non-local, CBAM, multi-head self-attention) across multiple CNN architectures (VGG, ResNet, Inception) [4]. Evaluation metrics vary by application, including accuracy measures for classification tasks [1,8], BLEU scores for reconstruction quality [7], and mean Average Precision for object detection [13].

## 4. RESEARCH GAPS

Several limitations and research gaps emerge from the literature. The attention collapse issue in deep Vision Transformers, where attention maps become similar in deeper layers, limits the scalability of pure Transformer approaches and necessitates techniques like Re-attention [19]. Computational complexity remains a concern, as self-attention mechanisms increase computational cost with image resolution, though approaches like Swin Transformer's shifted window design can reduce complexity to linearity [13].

The integration of CNN and Transformer architectures presents ongoing challenges. While feature modulation approaches show promise [6], the optimal methods for combining these architectures remain an active research area. Additionally, the effectiveness of attention mechanisms varies significantly across different tasks, with spatial attention performing best for classification while channel attention excels in regression problems [4].

Understanding of attention mechanisms in vision models requires further investigation. Recent analysis suggests that self-attention in Vision Transformers performs perceptual grouping rather than true attention, grouping figures based on visual feature similarity rather than exhibiting human-like attention effects [14]. This finding challenges conventional understanding and suggests room for improvement in attention mechanism design [17].

## 5. CONCLUSION

The literature demonstrates that CNNs remain highly effective for computer vision tasks, with performance significantly enhanced through integration with attention mechanisms and Transformer architectures. Hybrid approaches that combine CNN's local representation capabilities with Transformer's global context modeling consistently outperform pure CNN or pure Transformer models across diverse applications. However, optimal integration strategies and the fundamental nature of attention mechanisms in vision models require continued research to fully realize their potential.
