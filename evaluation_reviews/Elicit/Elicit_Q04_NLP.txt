Building on our earlier discussion of transformers, BERT, and GPT as foundational architectures, the NLP landscape has shifted rapidly in the last two years across several fronts. My search surfaced recent surveys and technical papers (mostly 2024
t2025) covering the key threads.

**Reasoning and chain-of-thought.** One of the most active areas is improving LLMs' ability to reason through multi-step problems. Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting 	extemdash where models generate intermediate reasoning steps 	extemdash has evolved considerably. A comprehensive review by Ferrag et al. (2025) covering 27 LLMs released between 2023 and 2025 (including DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI o1/o3, Qwen-32B) highlights techniques like inference-time compute scaling, reinforcement learning, and distillation as the main drivers of reasoning improvements. <citations>0</citations> Newer approaches combine CoT with retrieval: CoT-RAG, for instance, uses knowledge graphs to guide reasoning chain generation and retrieval-augmented generation together, reporting accuracy gains of 4
dash44% over prior methods on reasoning benchmarks. <citations>1</citations> Another line of work ("Chain of Evidences") replaces unverified reasoning claims with evidence explicitly extracted from the context, achieving a new state-of-the-art of 53.8% on LogiQA with GPT-4, surpassing standard CoT by 18%. <citations>2</citations>

**Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).** RAG has moved from a simple "retrieve then generate" pattern to more sophisticated iterative architectures. RetroRAG introduces retroactive reasoning, where the system revises and updates evidence during the reasoning process rather than committing to an irreversible forward chain. <citations>3</citations> SearChain interleaves search and reasoning by generating a "Chain-of-Query" where each reasoning node triggers a verification retrieval, improving traceability and accuracy on multi-hop QA and fact-checking tasks. <citations>4</citations> RAG is increasingly seen as essential infrastructure for LLMs 	extemdash a 2026 discussion paper from IEEE ICDE frames it as addressing three core LLM limitations: constrained multi-step reasoning under next-token prediction, reliance on static pre-training data, and lack of built-in factual verification. <citations>5</citations>

**Multimodal (vision-language) models.** The integration of vision and language has accelerated substantially. A survey by Li et al. (2025) covering VLMs from 2019
dash2024 notes that models like CLIP, Claude, and GPT-4V now beat classical single-modality vision models on zero-shot classification. <citations>6</citations> Aya Vision, released in 2025, demonstrates multilingual multimodal capabilities 	extemdash the 8B model outperforms Qwen-2.5-VL-7B, Pixtral-12B, and even the much larger LLaMA-3.2-90B-Vision, while a 32B version outperforms models more than twice its size. <citations>7</citations> A key technical challenge remains aligning visual and textual modalities without degrading text-only capabilities 	extemdash Aya Vision addresses this with cross-modal model merging to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. <citations>7</citations>

**Alignment: RLHF and beyond.** Aligning LLMs with human values remains a central research challenge. A heavily cited survey by Wang et al. (2024) catalogs the full landscape of alignment techniques, from foundational RLHF and PPO to newer approaches like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which simplifies the RLHF pipeline by eliminating the need for a separate reward model. <citations>8</citations> A 2025 technical survey by Srivastava & Aggarwal notes that RLHF remains dominant for alignment, while outcome-based RL (RLVR) significantly improves stepwise reasoning, though challenges like reward hacking and computational costs persist. <citations>9</citations> Instruction tuning 	extemdash fine-tuning LLMs on instruction-response pairs 	extemdash has also matured, with recent work emphasizing that the *alignment between instructions and responses* matters more than the individual quality of either component. <citations>10</citations>

**Efficiency and architecture innovations.** While not the strongest thread in my search results, several papers mention mixture-of-experts (MoE) configurations, state space models (like Mamba) as alternatives to transformers for long-context processing, parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA, and inference-time compute scaling as key efficiency-oriented advances. <citations>0,11</citations>
